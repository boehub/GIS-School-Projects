# Chesapeake Bay Wetlands and Flood Hazard

**Course:** GEOG 101S · **Tool:** ArcGIS Online · **Live map:** https://arcg.is/0z1zjC0

## Question
How do wetlands and flood hazard relate along the southern Chesapeake Bay, including Hampton Roads?

## Data
Esri imagery and reference layers for the southern Chesapeake region. The assignment called for the US Fish and Wildlife Service wetlands layer and FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer; I could not locate those exact layers (they appear to have been moved or removed), so I used Esri imagery as an alternative visual reference.

## What the map shows
- Five wetland systems occur in the area: **estuarine, lacustrine, marine, palustrine, and riverine**. Palustrine (marsh, swamp, and other freshwater wetlands) is the largest in the area shown.
- Wetlands act as natural sponges. They absorb stormwater and release it slowly, which slows flow and lowers flood peaks.
- Chesapeake and parts of Virginia Beach have extensive marsh and swamp land cut by rivers such as the North Landing River.
- Wetlands and flood-prone areas appear to overlap in places, while some low-lying developed areas have little wetland cover.
- **Norfolk** is a clear example of a flood-prone city whose landscape has been heavily urbanized and industrialized. Paved surfaces absorb far less water than wet soil, so runoff reaches drains and waterways faster.

## Limitations and next steps
- **The flood-hazard conclusions are visual.** Without the FEMA flood hazard layer I could not directly test the overlap between wetlands and flood zones. Norfolk's flooding is also driven by sea-level rise and land subsidence, not only lost wetlands.
- With the right layers (for example, the Living Atlas flood hazard and wetlands layers), I would run an overlay and calculate the percentage of flood-zone area that is wetland, by city, to turn the impression into a number.

## Skills shown
Reading environmental layers, handling missing data with a documented substitution, connecting land cover to flood behavior, local (Hampton Roads) context.
